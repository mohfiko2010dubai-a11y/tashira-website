    THE INTENDED BEHAVIOUR IS SETTLED — the Assistant's own family panel says

A5. THE EXPRESS WINDOW — DECIDED.  (owner delegated this decision)

    Implement:

      REGULAR  "We submit your application within 48 hours of your documents
                being complete. Issuing time is decided by the authority."
               «نقدّم طلبك خلال 48 ساعة من اكتمال مستنداتك. تحدد الجهة المختصة
                موعد إصدار التأشيرة.»

      EXPRESS  "Priority handling — we submit within 24 hours of your
                documents being complete, or we refund the express fee in

               «معالجة ذات أولوية — نقدّم طلبك خلال 24 ساعة من اكتمال
                مستنداتك، وإلا رددنا رسم الاستعجال كاملًا. تحدد الجهة المختصة
                موعد إصدار التأشيرة.»

    Both windows are ROUND THE CLOCK. No "working hours" qualifier, no
    timezone caveat, no weekend exception — the company operates 24 hours and
    A3 exists to remove exactly that language. A window that needs a
    definition is a window that will be argued about.

    WHY THESE NUMBERS, since the previous copy said 6 hours:

    The dangerous promise was never express. Express is opt-in and a minority
    of orders. REGULAR at 24 hours applied to every single order, unguarded,
    and that is the promise that was carrying the operational risk. Loosening
    the universal promise and keeping a tight one only where we have been paid
    to be fast is how the pressure comes off without losing the selling point.

    24 hours is chosen for express because it is what one person can hit by
    checking twice a day with a full margin of error: the real worst case at
    two checks a day is about 12 hours, so the guarantee carries 100% buffer.
    A 12-hour promise would have had none, and every slip is now an automatic
    refund.

    Even at these numbers we are ahead: gccev guarantees 24 hours at $50,
    GOVR guarantees 36 hours at $50 and admits it may reach 48 over a weekend.
    We match the fastest of them, at $30, and neither of them offers a refund
    guarantee prominently. Our regular at 48 hours still beats GOVR's regular
    of "1 to 3 working days", which is up to five calendar days across a
    weekend.

    REVISIT AFTER 50 ORDERS, using the completion and submission timestamps
    that TASK 15 §5 requires. If the real median is comfortably under 12
    hours, tighten express then. A promise you improve later is a marketing
    event; a promise you retreat from is damage. Start conservative.

   is worse than saying nothing.

   The homepage says the application is open 24 hours (A3). The WhatsApp

   time. Any internal response-time target the owner keeps stays internal.

4. LICENCE ACTIVITIES — CLOSED. The owner has confirmed the licence carries
   tourism and government-services activities — the same basis the
   competitors operate on, not a travel-agency licence.

   This unblocks the /verify page in D4, and it changes its priority: the
   licensed-activities list is now publishable, and it is the strongest
   verifiable trust element this business has, because most of what it
   competes against on price is unlicensed. Move D4 to the front of Section D.

   NOTE: this is also why B9a matters. The 90-day product is the one place
   where our own pricing does not follow our own logic, and it sits in the
   same list as the licence number and the guarantee. Trust signals are read
   together.

5. RESTRICTED NATIONALITIES — APPROVED, IMPLEMENT.

   Starting list: Iran, South Sudan, Uganda, Democratic Republic of the Congo.

   Shown BEFORE payment, worded as "Applications are not currently available
   for nationals of…" / «التقديم غير متاح حاليًا لمواطني…» — not "banned".
   Checkout is blocked for those nationalities, with a "contact us" route
   instead of a dead end. Blocking before payment prevents a full refund and
   an angry customer, which costs more than the lost order.

   Store this as configuration the owner can edit, not as code. NOTE FOR THE
   RECORD: this list is taken from a competitor's published notice and has not
   been independently verified; it changes over time. It is a starting point
   and the owner should confirm it against his own source.

6. VAT — DECIDED, NO CHANGE. The switch stays off, the company is not
   registered, and nothing about VAT appears on the site. The threshold
   monitor stays as built and alerts at AED 300,000 of trailing revenue, ahead
   of the AED 375,000 mandatory threshold. Do not build anything further here.

7. FEE ITEMISATION — DECIDED: NO. The price stays a single total described as
   "Government fees included" / «شامل الرسوم الحكومية», and we do not split it.

   THE STANDING RULE THAT COMES WITH THAT DECISION, and it is a hard one:

     NO SPECIFIC GOVERNMENT-FEE AMOUNT IS PUBLISHED ANYWHERE. Not on a page,
     not in the price cards, not in the terms, not in the refund policy, not
     in the Assistant, not in the invoice or proforma, not in any email
     template, and not in an admin-facing field that can print onto a customer
     document.

   I checked the live build for this: the phrase appears only as a category —
   "Government fees included" on the price cards, and «الرسوم الحكومية أو رسوم
   الموردين المتكبدة فعلياً» in the Arabic refund policy. No figure appears
   anywhere. That is the correct state and it must be preserved.

   Two consequences to implement:

     a. Add a check to the build or the review checklist: any new copy, invoice
        field or template that would print a government-fee amount is rejected.
        This is easy to reintroduce by accident the first time someone builds
        a "fee breakdown" into an invoice.
     b. The Arabic refund policy says non-refundable deductions include
        government or supplier fees "actually incurred" (المتكبدة فعلياً). That
        wording is good and must not change — it commits to no number and
        permits no deduction beyond what was really paid. Keep the Finance
        records per order accurate enough to support it if a refund is ever
        disputed.
